# Quiet Hours privacy policy

Effective date: 9 November 2026

Quiet Hours helps you spend less time in the apps you choose. You set quiet hours, iOS closes those apps in them, and a badge stays lit while you keep them. Once each evening the app asks how the day went, reads the health data your phone already has, and after three weeks shows what moved with your days. This page says exactly what the app keeps, where, and what leaves your phone. The short version: everything stays on your phone, and nothing leaves it.

## What the app stores

**The apps you choose.** You pick apps in Apple's picker, and iOS counts your minutes in each of them, in 5-minute steps; Quiet Hours stores one number per app per day. The app does not know which apps you picked: iOS hands it an opaque token for each one, and only iOS can draw the app's name and icon on screen. Inside Quiet Hours each app is just "App 1", "App 2" and so on. The app is never told what you did inside any app, only when your daily total crossed a 5-minute mark.

**Your plan.** Up to three quiet-hours windows, each with its hours, the days it runs, which of your chosen apps it closes, how many breaks it allows and how long each one lasts. A change that loosens a window while it runs is kept beside the plan until it takes effect.

**Quiet hours.** In each window, Quiet Hours asks iOS to show a Quiet hours screen over that window's apps. That is a Screen Time restriction on your own phone, set by you alone. What you write when you ask for a break is not kept.

**One small record per day.** For each window: whether it started and ended, how many breaks you took and when the last one started and ended, and whether you rang the bell to give the window up, and at what time. Also the times you asked for a break that day. The Good day badge widget reads these records to draw itself.

**Your evening answers.** Each time you answer the evening question or fill in the Check in tab, the app stores the time, how you felt (1 to 10), your energy if you rated it (1 to 10), how well you spent the day if you rated it (1 to 10), any tags you tapped (such as Work, People or Outside) and any note you typed.

**Daily Health metrics.** If you connect the Health app, Quiet Hours copies one number per day for each category you allow: hours of sleep, steps, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, active energy, exercise minutes and workout minutes. The first sync reads the last 90 days; later syncs read recent days. It never writes to Health.

**A few settings.** Whether you have seen the introduction, whether sample data is switched on, whether Health is connected and when it last synced, the step size app counting uses, and whether the two reminders are on.

**Use counts.** How many times each tab and each feature was used, and whether the introduction was read to the end or skipped, as numbers only. They are behind the Usage summary page under Settings, which shows them as a short text you can send if you choose, together with the state of quiet hours at that moment: whether a window is running, how many apps it should close and how many are closed, whether a break is on, and when the apps were last closed.

Earlier versions had a field for typing your total screen time by hand. If you used it, those numbers are still stored for the days you entered them.

## Where it is stored

On your phone, in the app's own storage, and in a small shared folder (an "App Group" container) that lets the app's six helper extensions work: one draws the evening question inside a notification and hands your answer to the app, one receives the app-usage counts from iOS, one draws the Good day badge on your Home or Lock Screen, one draws the Screen time box on Insights from the phone's own Screen Time data, and two draw the Quiet hours screen and handle its buttons. All are part of Quiet Hours and run only on your phone.

The app does not use iCloud. Your phone's own backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) include Quiet Hours's data in the same way they include every other app's, under Apple's encryption and your Apple account.

## What leaves your phone

Nothing. Quiet Hours has no server and no account. It does not use the network. It contains no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code that reports anything. The developer cannot see your data.

The one exception is outside the app: if you have turned on "Share with App Developers" under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, Apple may send the developer a crash report when the app crashes. Those reports come from Apple, contain technical details about the crash, and contain none of your answers, Health data or app counts.

The other exception is yours to make. The Usage summary page under Settings shows the use counts as a short text and offers to send it. It goes only where you send it, through the share sheet, and only when you tap Send. It contains no answers, no Health numbers, no app names and no minutes.

## Permissions, and how to withdraw each one

Quiet Hours asks for three permissions, each from the screen that needs it, and works with whichever you grant.

**Screen Time**, so iOS can close the apps you pick in quiet hours and count your minutes in them. The Screen time box on the Insights tab is drawn by a sealed iOS extension from the phone's own Screen Time data; those numbers never reach Quiet Hours's storage. To withdraw: Settings > Screen Time, under Apps with Screen Time Access, turn off Quiet Hours; quiet hours then stop closing anything. To stop counting one app, open the Plan page in Quiet Hours (the Today tab, then Plan) and swipe the app away; the days already counted are kept, and the app's token is kept so its history keeps its column.

**Notifications**, for the evening question and the two reminders (before a window starts and before a break ends). To withdraw: Settings > Notifications > Quiet Hours, or Settings > Apps > Quiet Hours > Notifications.

**Health (read only)**, so it can copy daily metrics. To withdraw or change categories: open the Health app, tap your picture, then Apps (or Apps and Services), then Quiet Hours. Numbers already copied stay in Quiet Hours until you delete them (below).

## How to delete everything

In the app, open Settings (the gear at the top of any tab) and tap Delete all data. That removes every answer, every copied Health day, every app count, the list of chosen apps and their tokens, your plan and its daily records, the shared folder and every setting, lifts every Quiet hours screen, and stops app counting, then shows the introduction again. The Health app keeps its own data, because it belongs to Health, not to Quiet Hours.

Deleting the app from your phone removes the same data.

## Not a medical device

Quiet Hours is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat or prevent anything. What it shows are statistical associations in your own data: that one measure tended to move with your days over the days it saw. An association is not a cause, and the app says so on screen. If you are struggling, please talk to a doctor or someone you trust.

## Age

Quiet Hours is not directed at children under 13. The app asks for no personal details and collects nothing from anyone.

## Sample data

The Settings screen has a "Show sample data" switch. It shows ninety made-up days, clearly marked "Sample data", so you can see what Insights look like before your own three weeks are in. The sample is built into the app, is never mixed with your data, and switches itself off once your own data reaches the minimum.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address with a new effective date, and the app's release notes for that version say that it changed. The app itself never sends anything, so a change can only ever be about what the app keeps on your phone.
