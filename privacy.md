# Feel Good privacy policy

Effective date: 2 November 2026

Feel Good is a mood diary for iPhone. It asks once each evening how you feel, how your energy was and how well the day went, reads the health data your phone already has, and after three weeks shows which of those signals move with your mood. This page says exactly what the app keeps, where, and what leaves your phone. The short version: everything stays on your phone, and nothing leaves it.

## What the app stores

**Your check-ins.** Each time you answer a prompt or fill in the Check in tab, the app stores the time, how you felt (1 to 10), your energy if you rated it (1 to 10), how well you spent the day if you rated it in the evening (1 to 10), any tags you tapped (such as Work, People or Outside), any note you typed, and which scheduled prompt you were answering.

**Daily Health metrics.** If you connect the Health app, Feel Good copies one number per day for each category you allow: hours of sleep, steps, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, active energy, exercise minutes and workout minutes. The first sync reads the last 90 days; later syncs read recent days. It never writes to Health.

**Time in apps.** If you turn on app tracking and pick apps in Apple's picker, iOS counts your minutes in each of those apps, in 5-minute steps, and Feel Good stores one number per app per day. The app does not know which apps you picked: iOS hands it an opaque token for each one, and only iOS can draw the app's name and icon on screen. Inside Feel Good each app is just "App 1", "App 2" and so on. The app is never told what you did inside any app, only when your daily total crossed a 5-minute mark.

**Quiet hours (optional).** If you turn on Close the apps, Feel Good asks iOS to show a Quiet hours screen over the apps you chose during the hours you set. That is a Screen Time restriction on your own phone, switched on and off by you alone. What you answer or write when you ask for time is not kept: the app holds only the pass itself, which app and for how many minutes, while it runs.

**The Good day badge (optional).** If you turn the badge on, the app stores its settings (the quiet hours, and for each window which of your tracked apps count in it, its allowance and its breaks) and one small record per day: whether quiet hours started, the minutes those apps were used together inside them in 5-minute steps, whether the badge was lost and at what time, whether you started the day again, when your last break of the day started and ended and how many you took, in all and per window, whether quiet hours ended, and the times you asked for an app, which set how long the next ask makes you wait. The badge widget reads these records to draw itself. The app may show you a notification when the allowance is used up and when the badge is lost; both are made on your phone.

**A few settings.** Whether you have seen the introduction, whether sample data is switched on, whether Health is connected and when it last synced, and the step size app tracking uses.

**Use counts.** How many times each tab and each feature was used, and whether the introduction was read to the end or skipped, as numbers only. They are behind the Usage summary page under Settings, which shows them as a short text you can send if you choose.

Earlier versions had a field for typing your total screen time by hand. If you used it, those numbers are still stored for the days you entered them.

## Where it is stored

On your phone, in the app's own storage, and in a small shared folder (an "App Group" container) that lets the app's six helper extensions work: one draws the check-in inside a notification and hands your answer to the app, one receives the app-usage counts from iOS, one draws the Good day badge on your Home or Lock Screen, one draws the Screen time box on Insights from the phone's own Screen Time data, and two draw the Quiet hours screen and handle its buttons. All are part of Feel Good and run only on your phone.

The app does not use iCloud. Your phone's own backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) include Feel Good's data in the same way they include every other app's, under Apple's encryption and your Apple account.

## What leaves your phone

Nothing. Feel Good has no server and no account. It does not use the network. It contains no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code that reports anything. The developer cannot see your data.

The one exception is outside the app: if you have turned on "Share with App Developers" under Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements, Apple may send the developer a crash report when the app crashes. Those reports come from Apple, contain technical details about the crash, and contain none of your check-ins, Health data or app counts.

The other exception is yours to make. The Usage summary page under Settings shows the use counts as a short text and offers to send it. It goes only where you send it, through the share sheet, and only when you tap Send. It contains no check-ins, no Health numbers, no app names and no minutes.

## Permissions, and how to withdraw each one

Feel Good asks for three permissions, each from the screen that needs it, and works with whichever you grant.

**Notifications**, so it can ask how you feel. To withdraw: Settings > Notifications > Feel Good, or Settings > Apps > Feel Good > Notifications.

**Health (read only)**, so it can copy daily metrics. To withdraw or change categories: open the Health app, tap your picture, then Apps (or Apps and Services), then Feel Good. Numbers already copied stay in Feel Good until you delete them (below).

**Screen Time**, so iOS can count minutes in the apps you pick. The Screen time box on the Insights tab is drawn by a sealed iOS extension from the phone's own Screen Time data; those numbers never reach Feel Good's storage. To withdraw: Settings > Screen Time, under Apps with Screen Time Access, turn off Feel Good. To stop counting one app, open the Plan page in Feel Good (the Today tab, then Plan) and swipe the app away; the days already counted are kept, and the app's token is kept so its history keeps its column.

## How to delete everything

In the app, open Settings (the gear at the top of any tab) and tap Delete all data. That removes every check-in, every copied Health day, every app count, the list of tracked apps and their tokens, the shared folder, every setting, and stops app tracking, then shows the introduction again. The Health app keeps its own data, because it belongs to Health, not to Feel Good.

Deleting the app from your phone removes the same data.

## Not a medical device

Feel Good is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat or prevent anything. What it shows are statistical associations in your own data: that one measure tended to move with your mood over the days it saw. An association is not a cause, and the app says so on screen. If you are struggling, please talk to a doctor or someone you trust.

## Age

Feel Good is not directed at children under 13 and is listed on the App Store with a 13+ age rating. The app asks for no personal details and collects nothing from anyone.

## Sample data

The Settings screen has a "Show sample data" switch. It shows ninety made-up days, clearly marked "Sample data", so you can see what Insights look like before your own three weeks are in. The sample is built into the app, is never mixed with your data, and switches itself off once your own data reaches the minimum.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version is published at this address with a new effective date, and the app's release notes for that version say that it changed. The app itself never sends anything, so a change can only ever be about what the app keeps on your phone.
