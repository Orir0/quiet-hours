# Quiet Hours support

Quiet Hours helps you spend less time in the apps you choose. Set quiet hours, and iOS closes those apps in them; a badge on your Home or Lock Screen stays lit while you keep them. Once each evening the app asks how the day went, and after three weeks it shows whether your quiet hours had anything to do with your better days. Everything stays on your phone; the [privacy policy](../privacy/) says exactly what is kept and where.

## What the app does

**Your first window.** After the introduction, Quiet Hours asks for Screen Time access, then helps you set your first window: the apps you most want to cut down on, the hours, how many breaks the window allows and how long each break lasts. A short animation plays a day through it so you can see how it works before you keep it.

**Quiet hours.** Up to ten windows, each with its hours, the days of the week it runs, which of your chosen apps it closes, and its own breaks. A morning and an evening window, or weekday and weekend hours, are both possible; a day with no window is a day off. A window may run past midnight, for bedtime; the night then counts for the day it starts on. Inside a window, the apps it closes open to a Quiet hours screen instead. All of it is on the Plan page (the Today tab, then Plan); tap a window to change it.

**Breaks.** Need one of the apps? Tap Take a break on the Quiet hours screen, or on the Today tab. The screen tells you how many are left ("1 of 2 breaks left") and asks what for. The window's apps then open for the break's length and close again by themselves; the badge stays lit. Saying what for is the point; the words are not kept.

**Ring the bell.** Used every break in a window and still want the apps? Wait for the window to end, or ring the bell: the window ends, its apps open until its end time, and today's Good day is lost. Quiet Hours asks you to confirm first, and there is no starting again after it.

**The Good day badge.** A widget for your Home or Lock Screen. It fills as each window passes, stays lit while you keep it, and seals when the window is over. It goes out only if you ring the bell. The Today tab shows the same day: each window with its state drawn as on the badge (sunrise before it, sun while it runs, a mug on a break, the seal once kept, a cloud if the bell was rung) and the breaks used, such as 2/2; and how yesterday went, in full in the morning and in one line after.

**A plan you cannot loosen in the moment.** A change that tightens your plan (more hours, fewer breaks, another app) starts at once. A change that loosens a window (shorter hours, more or longer breaks, an app removed, the window deleted) starts at once too, unless that window is running or starts within the hour; then it waits for that run to end. The Plan page shows what is waiting and when it lands, and lets you drop it.

**Time in apps.** iOS counts your minutes in each app you chose, in 5-minute steps. Each app becomes a line under Your data on Insights and a line in Trends, drawn day by day under a mark at 10 minutes to stay beneath.

**Screen time.** The Insights tab opens with a Screen time box drawn by iOS: today's total time on the phone, pickups and notifications, the last seven days as bars, today's top apps, and the last seven days against the seven before, app by app, so you can watch the number go down. These numbers are drawn by iOS inside a sealed view; Quiet Hours cannot read, store or test them, which is why they are shown but not analysed.

**The evening question.** One notification a day, at a random minute between six and nine in the evening. Hold it to answer from the lock screen: how you feel, your energy and how well you spent the day, each from 1 to 10. The Check in tab asks the same, with optional tags and a note; the day question appears there from 17:00.

**Health data (optional).** After you connect the Health app, Quiet Hours copies one number per day for sleep, steps, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, active energy, exercise minutes and workouts. It syncs by itself whenever you open the app or save an answer.

## How insights work

Insights need 21 days that have both a mood rating and at least one other number. With fewer days the tab shows a progress bar and nothing else, because anything it said earlier would be noise.

Once there are enough days, the app compares each daily measure, and each measure's value from the day before, with how you felt and how well you said the day was spent. It uses a rank correlation, so a few unusual days do not dominate, and it corrects for the fact that it is testing many measures at once, so a pattern has to be strong enough to survive that correction before it is shown. Each pattern says how strong it is, which direction it runs, and how many days it is based on.

These are associations, not causes. "Less time in App 2 goes with better days" means those two moved together in your data over those days. It does not mean one caused the other; something else may drive both. Treat a pattern as a question worth asking about your own life, not as an answer.

## Common questions

**I opened one of my apps in quiet hours and nothing happened.**
Check three things. That the window has apps: open it on the Plan page and see that its apps are switched on. That you picked single apps, not a whole category: in Apple's picker, tap the arrow beside a category and tick the apps inside it, because a ticked category on its own is not kept. And that Screen Time access is on: Settings > Screen Time > Apps with Screen Time Access. If all three are right, open Quiet Hours once; it puts the Quiet hours screen back in place.

**Can I take a break without losing the badge?**
Yes. Breaks are what keep the badge lit when you need an app. Each window has its own number of breaks and its own break length. You can end a break early from the Today tab.

**What happens when my breaks are used?**
The Quiet hours screen says "No breaks left": the apps open again when the window ends. If you can't wait, ring the bell. The window ends, its apps open, and today's Good day is lost. Tomorrow starts fresh.

**How do I stop quiet hours?**
Switch a window's apps off or delete the window on the Plan page. Like any loosening, it waits while that window is running or starts within the hour. Turning off Quiet Hours under Settings > Screen Time > Apps with Screen Time Access stops it at once.

**Will it remind me?**
Two reminders, both on by default under Reminders on the Plan page: one five minutes before a window starts, and one a minute before a break ends so you can finish what you were doing. Both need notifications allowed.

**Why does app counting start the next morning?**
iOS counts an app's minutes from the start of each day. The day you pick an app is usually left blank rather than guessed, because part of it went uncounted; from the next morning every day is counted. A day shows 0 when the app was being counted and you did not reach the first 5-minute mark, and shows nothing when iOS was not counting that day.

**How do I stop counting an app?**
On the Plan page, swipe the app away. iOS stops counting it at once, and it is no longer closed in any window. The days already counted are kept so its history is not lost.

**Why is a Health metric blank?**
Yesterday's or today's value is blank when Health has no number for that day. Common reasons: that category is not allowed for Quiet Hours (check the Health app, your picture, then Apps, then Quiet Hours); nothing recorded it (heart rate, heart rate variability and exercise minutes mostly come from an Apple Watch; sleep needs a watch or a sleep app); or the day has not synced yet (Sync now in Settings forces it). A category that is blank on every day is not shown.

**I said no to a permission. How do I change my mind?**
Each screen tells you when its permission is off and has an Open Settings button. Screen Time: Settings > Screen Time > Apps with Screen Time Access. Notifications: Settings > Notifications > Quiet Hours. Health: the Health app, your picture, Apps, Quiet Hours.

**What is "Show sample data"?**
A switch in Settings that shows ninety made-up days, marked "Sample data" on screen, so you can see what Insights and History look like before you have three weeks of your own. It is built into the app, never mixed with your data, and turns itself off once your own data reaches 21 days.

**How do I delete everything?**
Settings (the gear at the top of any tab) > Delete all data. It removes every answer, Health copy, app count, your plan and every setting from your phone, lifts the Quiet hours screens and stops app counting. Deleting the app does the same. The Health app keeps its own data.

**Does anything leave my phone?**
No. There is no account, no server, no analytics and no advertising. Details are in the [privacy policy](../privacy/).
